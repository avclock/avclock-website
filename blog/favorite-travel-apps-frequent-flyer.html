The Apps I Actually Use as a Frequent Flyer

5 min read

A few of the links below are referral or affiliate links. If you sign up through one, I may earn a small reward or discount at no extra cost to you. I only share things I actually use and would recommend anyway.

As someone who flies standby often across a few different airlines, my phone's home screen is basically a survival kit at this point. These are the apps that have actually earned a permanent spot on it, not because anyone's paying me to say so, but because I open them constantly.

FlightConnections, for Finding Routes That Actually Work

Try FlightConnections

When you're flying standby across United, Delta, Alaska, and American, the question isn't "does a flight exist," it's "which flight actually has room." FlightConnections is the tool I open first to answer that. The free version already maps direct routes between any two airports worldwide, which is useful on its own. The premium tier is what makes it a daily habit: you can filter by alliance or specific airline, see which carriers fly a route and how often, check seasonal route data instead of guessing, and save searches so you're not rebuilding the same route comparison every time plans change. For anyone piecing together a standby itinerary across multiple airlines, that's the feature set that actually matters.

Flighty, for Tracking Every Flight

Get Flighty

Flighty tracks flights better than the airline apps do, full stop. It'll flag a likely delay before the airline officially announces it, tell you which gate and tail number you're actually getting, and quietly log your flying history in the background: total flights, miles flown, hours in the air, and total delay time across the year. If you like seeing your own travel stats add up, it's oddly satisfying, and the Lock Screen and widget support means you can glance at a flight's status without opening the app at all.

AvClock, for Everything Between Flights

Get AvClock

I'm obviously biased here, but this is genuinely why I built it. Between flights, the questions I actually have are: what time is it where I'm going, what's the weather doing at that airport right now, and is anything going on with the airspace I should know about. AvClock's free tier covers local and Zulu time for any airport worldwide with live weather in plain English. Premium adds the stuff frequent flyers actually want: live METAR/TAF weather with color-coded VFR/MVFR/IFR/LIFR badges, FAA ground stop and delay alerts, per-airport weather alerts you can mute individually, a My Flights log with calendar reminders, a Time Converter for shifting every clock together, heliports and hospital helipads, and Live Activities that keep flight category, temperature, and wind live on your Lock Screen and Dynamic Island. It's a one-time $4.99 unlock, not a subscription.

Monarch, for Actually Understanding Where My Money Goes

Try Monarch

Travel gets expensive in ways that are easy to lose track of across multiple cards and accounts. Monarch pulls everything into one place with real budgeting and net worth tracking that doesn't feel like a spreadsheet. It's genuinely changed how I think about spending, travel and otherwise. That link gets you 50% off your first year, a $50 value, if you want to try it.

Sumizeit, for Layovers

Try Sumizeit

Long layovers are dead time unless you plan for them. Sumizeit gives you quick, well-produced audio summaries of books, which is a much better use of a two-hour layover than doomscrolling. They're currently running a full year of premium free through that link.

Capital One Shopping, for Money I'd Otherwise Leave on the Table

Try Capital One Shopping

This one isn't travel-specific, but it's become a habit for basically every online purchase I make. Capital One Shopping automatically checks for coupons and cashback at checkout. I've earned over $632 through it in about a year of just letting it run in the background, which is the kind of "free money" that's easy to dismiss until you actually add it up.

The Widgets Tie It All Together

The apps that have earned real estate on my Home Screen widgets are the ones I actually check without opening: AvClock for airport time and weather at a glance, the United app for my next flight's status, Flighty for a second opinion on delays, and Monarch for a quick net-worth check. If an app doesn't have a widget worth using, it's competing for a tap I'd rather not spend.

That's the actual list, not a sponsored roundup, just what's still on my phone after trying a lot of alternatives. If you're a frequent flyer piecing together your own setup, that's a solid place to start.
