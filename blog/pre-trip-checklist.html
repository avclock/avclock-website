The Ultimate Pre-Trip Checklist: Currency, Plugs, Time Zones, and What Else to Check Before You Fly

5 min read

The flight is booked, the bag is packed, and it still feels like something's missing. Usually it's one of a handful of small, boring details that are easy to overlook and mildly painful to discover at the destination. Here's the full list, in the order it's worth checking it.

Entry Requirements: Passport, Visa, and Health Rules

Start here, since it's the one category that can actually stop a trip. Check your passport's expiration date: many countries require at least six months of validity remaining from your entry date, not your return date, which catches people off guard more often than it should. Confirm whether your destination requires a visa, an e-visa, or an electronic travel authorization arranged in advance, since requirements vary by nationality and can change with little notice. If you're connecting through a third country, check that country's transit rules too: some require their own visa even if you never leave the airport. Any destination-specific health requirements (vaccination proof, health declarations) are worth a quick check as well.

Currency and Payment

Figure out the local currency and roughly what things cost before you land, so unfamiliar prices don't feel like a math problem at the register. Notify your bank and credit card companies of travel dates if they ask for it, since an unexpected charge from a new country is a common reason cards get frozen mid-trip. Check whether your card charges a foreign transaction fee, and carry a small amount of local cash for places that don't take cards: some transit systems, markets, and smaller vendors still expect it.

Power Plugs and Voltage

Plug shapes and voltage vary by region, and neither one is obvious from where you're starting. Look up the specific plug type for your destination (there are more than a dozen in use worldwide) and pack the right adapter, ideally more than one if you're traveling with several devices. Voltage matters more than the plug shape: most modern laptop and phone chargers handle both 110-120V and 220-240V automatically, but older electronics, hair tools, and some small appliances don't, and running one on the wrong voltage can damage it. Check the label on the device itself, it'll usually say the input range right on the charger.

Time Zones and Scheduling

It's easy to book a connecting flight, hotel check-in, or meeting without noticing what time zone the confirmation is actually in. Double-check the local time at your destination against your departure time, especially for red-eye flights and tight connections, since a flight that "lands the next morning" can mean very different things depending on how many zones it crosses. This is also where it's worth confirming your layover airport's local time separately from your final destination's, since a short connection can look comfortable on paper and feel much tighter once you account for the zone change.

A Few Things People Forget

Rounding out the list: check whether your destination drives on the other side of the road if you're renting a car, confirm your phone plan covers international data or line up a local SIM/eSIM in advance, download offline maps and any translation apps before you lose reliable signal, and check the weather forecast close to departure rather than relying on the seasonal average, since a "sunny" destination can still surprise you.
