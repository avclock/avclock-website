What Is a Ground Stop? And What a Nationwide One Means for Your Flight

5 min read

On the afternoon of Friday, November 13, 2026, the FAA stopped departures across the United States. Its Command Center issued an advisory titled "National Ground Stop" at 2054Z (3:54 p.m. Eastern) and a second, "Nationwide Ground Stop," at 2246Z, giving the reason as a communications failure. If you were at a gate that afternoon, here is what that phrase means and what it does and doesn't tell you about your own flight.

What a Ground Stop Is

A ground stop is an order from air traffic control that holds flights on the ground at the airports they are leaving from. The usual kind covers one airport: if thunderstorms close in on Newark, the FAA stops flights bound for Newark wherever they are, in Chicago, Atlanta, or Denver, so they don't take off toward an airport that can't land them. Flights already in the air usually keep going.

Ground stops are meant to be short. Most last from 30 minutes to a couple of hours, and the FAA extends or shortens them as things change. Common reasons are weather, an equipment problem, a runway closure, or more traffic than the airport can handle.

Ground Stop, Ground Delay, and Closure

These three get mixed up, and they mean different things for your day.

Ground stopFlights to the airport are held at their departure airports until further noticeYour flight waits. It may leave soon after the stop lifts
Ground delay programFlights still go, each with a later assigned departure timeA delay with a number on it, often 30 minutes to a few hours
Arrival or departure delayTraffic is moving, but slowlyExpect to leave or land late

What Makes a Nationwide Ground Stop Different

A nationwide ground stop holds departures at every U.S. airport at once. It's rare. Before this one, the best-known examples were September 11, 2001, and the morning of January 11, 2023, when the system that distributes NOTAMs failed and the FAA paused departures for about an hour and a half.

A nationwide stop also ends differently. One airport can reopen all at once, but the whole country can't. On November 13 the FAA's advisories said flights would be released as its facilities recovered, a few at a time, with more traffic allowed as limits eased. So when you hear "the ground stop is lifted," your own flight may still be hours from leaving, and crews and aircraft will be out of position into the next day.

What to Do If Your Flight Is Caught in One

  • Trust your airline's app over the departure board. The FAA decides when traffic can move. Your airline decides which flights go, in what order, and which are cancelled.
  • Don't assume a cancellation. Many flights leave late once a stop lifts. Stay near the gate unless your airline tells you otherwise.
  • Rebook in the app first. During a big disruption the phone line and the counter are the slowest routes. Most airlines let you change flights in the app at no charge when a waiver is in effect.
  • Know your refund right. If your flight is cancelled and you choose not to travel, U.S. rules entitle you to a refund, not only a credit.
  • Check the airport you're flying to as well. A stop at your destination holds you at your origin, even if the weather outside your window is perfect.

Where to See Ground Stops Yourself

The FAA publishes airport delays at nasstatus.faa.gov: ground stops, ground delay programs, closures, and general delays, airport by airport. Nationwide actions are announced separately, in the Command Center's advisories at fly.faa.gov. Times on both are in Zulu.

Common Questions

What is an FAA ground stop?

A ground stop is an order from air traffic control that holds flights bound for an airport, or a region, on the ground at the airports they are leaving from. Flights already in the air usually continue.

How long does a ground stop last?

Most ground stops for a single airport last from 30 minutes to a couple of hours, and the FAA often extends or shortens them as conditions change. A nationwide ground stop is rare and has no usual length.

What is the difference between a ground stop and a ground delay program?

A ground stop holds flights until further notice. A ground delay program lets flights go, but gives each one a later departure time so arrivals reach the airport at a rate it can handle.

Will my flight be cancelled during a ground stop?

Not always. Many flights leave late once the stop lifts. Airlines decide cancellations flight by flight, so your airline's app is the place to check.
