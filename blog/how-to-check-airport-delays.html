How to Check Airport Delays Before You Leave Home

4 min read

The departure board at the airport is the last place to learn about a delay. Most of what it will tell you is knowable from your kitchen, an hour or three earlier, when you can still do something about it. Here are five places to look, in the order that saves the most time.

1. Your Airline's App

Start here, because it's the only source that knows about your flight in particular. Turn on notifications for the trip. Look for a changed departure time, a gate change, and whether a travel waiver has been posted for your airports, which usually means free changes to another flight.

2. The FAA's Airport Status Page

For U.S. airports, the FAA lists what's slowing each airport right now at nasstatus.faa.gov. Check the airport you're leaving from, the one you're flying to, and any connection. What the terms mean:

You seeIt means
Ground stopFlights to that airport are being held at their departure airports. Usually short
Ground delay programFlights to that airport get a later departure time. The page gives an average and a maximum delay
Departure or arrival delayTraffic is moving slowly, with a range such as 31 to 45 minutes and whether it's growing
Airport closureRead the text. Many apply only to private or unscheduled aircraft, not airline flights

The one that surprises people is a delay at home caused by somewhere else. If your destination has a ground stop, your flight waits at your origin, under a clear sky.

3. Where Your Plane Is Coming From

Your aircraft is usually flying another leg before yours. If that flight is late, yours will be too, and it shows hours ahead. Most airline apps have a "Where is my plane?" link on the flight status screen. If the inbound flight hasn't left yet and it's two hours away, your on-time departure isn't happening, whatever the board says.

4. The Weather at Both Ends

Thunderstorms, low cloud, snow, and strong crosswinds cut how many planes an airport can land each hour. Check your destination and your connection, not only where you are. Airports report their own weather in METAR and TAF reports, and the forecast (the TAF) covers the next 24 hours or more, which makes it useful the night before.

5. The Airport's Own Site

The airport knows things the FAA page doesn't: security wait times, a closed parking garage, construction at the terminal, a roadway backup. It's a 30-second check that mostly matters on holidays.

When to Check

  • The night before: weather forecasts at your destination and connection, and any airline waiver.
  • About three hours out: your inbound plane, and the FAA status for all your airports.
  • Before you leave home: your airline's app one more time.

Common Questions

How do I check if an airport has delays right now?

For U.S. airports, the FAA lists current ground stops, ground delay programs, closures, and general delays at nasstatus.faa.gov. Your airline's app shows what those mean for your own flight.

Why is my flight delayed when the weather is fine?

The delay is often somewhere else: at your destination, at the airport your plane is coming from, or along the route. The FAA holds flights at their departure airport when the arrival airport can't take them.

How early should I check for delays?

Check the night before for weather at your destination and connection, again about three hours before departure for your inbound plane, and once more before you leave home.
