Why United Airlines Became My New Favorite Airline to Fly

4 min read

For most of my flying life, Delta was the default. Not because I'd ever really compared it against anything else, just because it's who I started with, and switching airlines felt like more hassle than it was worth. That changed in the last year or so, when United quietly became the airline I actually look forward to flying. Here's what actually shifted my opinion, not marketing copy, just what I noticed after enough flights to trust the pattern.

The Small Things Add Up Fast

The first thing I noticed was Bluetooth. A lot of United's fleet now supports pairing your own headphones directly to the seatback screen, which sounds minor until you've spent a flight untangling a wired adapter or giving up on the in-flight entertainment entirely. If you travel with a good pair of noise-cancelling headphones (more on which ones are worth it in a future post), being able to pair them straight to the screen instead of fighting a 3.5mm jack is a genuinely nice upgrade, and it's the kind of detail that tells you an airline is actually investing in the cabin experience rather than just the livery.

Built for How Standby Flying Actually Works

If you fly standby with any regularity, route density matters more than almost any other factor. A hub-heavy network with frequent departures on the routes you actually need gives you real options when a flight doesn't work out, instead of one shot a day. United's domestic route map has consistently given me a backup plan when I needed one, which is the difference between standby being a stress-free perk and a gamble.

Pricing That Hasn't Felt Like a Trap

This one is purely anecdotal, but across a year of comparing fares for the same routes, United has more often than not come in at a fair price without the fee creep that makes a "cheap" fare balloon by the time you've picked a seat and checked a bag. Your mileage will vary by route and season, but it's been a consistent enough pattern that it's part of why I book United first now.

Free Starlink Wi-Fi Changed Short Flights

United has been rolling out Starlink-powered Wi-Fi across its fleet, and on the routes that have it, it's free and genuinely fast, not the "technically works" in-flight Wi-Fi most of us are used to tolerating. On a short flight, that's the difference between a dead hour and actually getting something done or just streaming something to unwind.

American Airlines: A Close Second

I'll give credit where it's due: American's fleet-wide partnership with AT&T for in-flight Wi-Fi has made it a strong second choice for me, especially on routes where United isn't the best option. Free, reliable connectivity across nearly the whole fleet is a real differentiator, and it's part of why American stays in regular rotation for me even though United has become the default.

None of this is a knock on Delta. It's just what actually moved the needle for me after enough flights to notice a real pattern instead of a one-off good or bad experience. If you're a frequent flyer weighing your own options, the small, consistent things (connectivity, route flexibility, and pricing that doesn't punish you at checkout) tend to matter a lot more than which airline has the flashiest ad campaign.
