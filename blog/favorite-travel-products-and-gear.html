The Travel Products and Gear I Actually Use

5 min read

One link below is a referral/affiliate link (noted where it applies) and I may earn a small reward if you use it, at no extra cost to you. Nothing here is medical or health advice, just what works for me; talk to a doctor before starting any supplement, especially if you take other medications.

None of this is sponsored beyond the one link I've flagged. It's just the gear that's survived enough trips to earn a permanent spot in my bag, after trying a lot of things that didn't.

Thorne Supplements, Especially 5-HTP

10% off Thorne

Thorne makes clean, third-party-tested supplements, and I don't travel without them. The one I reach for most is 5-HTP, which a lot of people use to support sleep and mood on disrupted travel schedules. It's genuinely worth reading up on before you start it, not just taking my word for it: MedlinePlus's overview of 5-HTP covers what it is, the evidence behind it, and real interaction warnings (it shouldn't be combined with SSRIs or other serotonin-affecting medications). That 10% link is the one affiliate link in this post; everything else here is just what I use.

Trtl Travel Pillow

See Trtl Travel

Not sponsored, just a genuinely good product. Most travel pillows are too soft to actually hold your neck in place, which matters if, like me, you've got a longer neck and need real support to actually sleep sitting up. The Trtl wraps around and holds its shape instead of collapsing, which is the whole difference between actually sleeping on a flight and just resting your eyes.

ASRV Clothing

See ASRV

Also not sponsored. ASRV's shirts and travel wear have a noticeably premium feel, and they hold up well whether you're working out or just living in them for a long travel day. They're not cheap at full price, but keeping an eye on resale sites like eBay for it has made it a lot more reasonable, and it still outlasts cheaper alternatives I've tried.

New Balance Shoes

For actual walking through airports, comfort wins over everything else. New Balance has been my go-to for a while now, comfortable enough for a long concourse walk and broken in enough by the time a trip actually starts that they're not fighting me the whole day.

The Tech I Actually Carry

A short list, since these don't need much explanation: AirPods Pro Max for noise cancelling that actually holds up on a full flight, a Steam Deck for long layovers, a MacBook Pro (always in a real case, always with a screen protector, a cracked screen mid-trip is not a problem worth risking), and an Apple Watch for the quick-glance stuff a phone is overkill for. None of this is cheap gear, but it's the kind of thing that actually earns its weight on a long travel day instead of just sitting in the bag.

That's the real list, not a sponsored roundup. If something's still in my bag after enough trips to actually wear it out or prove itself, that's the bar it had to clear.
